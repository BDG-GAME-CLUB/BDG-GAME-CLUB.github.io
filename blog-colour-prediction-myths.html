Five colour prediction
myths, explained.

Patterns can feel persuasive, especially during a fast session. Feeling convincing is not the same as predicting an uncertain outcome.

Five mythsStreaks predictPaid signalsDouble to recoverA secret algorithmProfit as income

Myth 1: a long streak makes the opposite colour “due”

A past sequence does not create a promise about the next round. The urge to balance a streak is a common thinking error, not proof.

Myth 2: paid tip groups have guaranteed signals

Screenshots can be selected after the fact, losses can be hidden and success rates can be invented. Treat every “100% win” claim as a red flag.

Myth 3: doubling after a loss guarantees recovery

Increasing stakes also increases the amount at risk. Limits, losing sequences and emotional decisions can turn a small loss into a serious one quickly.

Myth 4: someone has cracked a secret algorithm

A person who cannot provide independently verifiable evidence should not be trusted with money or account access. Never install tools or share credentials for a supposed predictor.

Myth 5: colour prediction is dependable income

Gaming should never be treated as wages, savings or debt repayment. Use only a fixed entertainment budget that can be lost without affecting essentials.

The safest strategy is a limit: decide the maximum time and money before starting, then stop when either limit is reached.
